## Status and manual acceptance

**MANUAL ACCEPTANCE: PASS for implemented Levels 1-7.** Neal manually tested
the 2.5-calibrated normal rim-pursuit behavior across Levels 1-7 and reported
that the levels feel right. The change materially addresses the original
stationary-defense problem: camping in one rim lane and single-tapping or
holding Fire no longer makes it extremely easy to destroy nearly every
Flipper after rim arrival. Chasing Flippers create meaningful pressure;
spinner movement, awareness and shot timing matter again. Temporary stationary
defense and careful shooting remain useful, and no regression was observed
across Levels 1-7.

The accepted normal rim rate is 2.5 times each level's former configured
Flipper turn rate. This is a **PROJECT DECISION / MANUAL CALIBRATION** based on
Neal's direct arcade comparison, not a literal multiplier established by the
Atari ROM. ROM-backed evidence remains limited to distinct top-of-tube Flipper
processing and level-derived top acceleration.

`--rim-speed <multiplier>` remains available as a diagnostic around the
accepted normal rate:

- No option and `--rim-speed 1` produce identical accepted behavior.
- `--rim-speed 0.4` approximately reproduces the former pre-calibration C#
  rim rate.
- Other positive finite values remain diagnostic multipliers.

The bounded master-ROM trace found no explicit adjacent-lane speed,
acceleration, delay or top-turn-factor branch, and no special faster pounce
state. The inspected flow continues ordinary top-rim movement until contact.
This is not proof about untraced parts of the ROM; geometry, directed-pivot
angle, contact timing or perception could explain the reported impression.
